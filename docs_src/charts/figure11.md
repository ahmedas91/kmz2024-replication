## Description

Replication of Figure 11 of Kelly, Malamud and Zhou (2024), Section V.E: variable importance (VI) for each of the 15 predictors, defined as the change in performance moving from the full 15-predictor model to a re-estimated 14-predictor model that excludes the variable, at the paper's Figure 11 setting (training window T = 12, P = 12,000 random features, shrinkage z = 10^3). Blue bars measure VI in out-of-sample R^2 units on the left axis; the orange line measures VI in annualized Sharpe ratio units on the right axis; variables are sorted descending by R^2 VI, as in the paper. Positive VI means the model performs worse without the variable.

The figure carries the paper's answer to "how can a 12-month training window be enough?": the model feeds on the fast-moving predictors. The replication reproduces the paper's top four in the paper's exact order — the lagged market return (R^2 VI 2.1% here vs the paper's 1.9%, Sharpe VI 0.13 vs the paper's ~0.13), long-term bond return ltr, default return dfr, then inflation — and, like the paper, puts the persistent valuation ratios near zero and svar, tbl, and ntis at the bottom. The middle of the ranking is statistically indistinguishable from zero and shuffles freely with seed noise.

Level notes: this cache averages 100 random-feature draws per configuration (the `FIG11_N_SEEDS` config) versus the paper's 1,000, which is why the fine ordering of the near-zero variables is noisy; `src/test_figure11.py` therefore asserts the paper's top-group/bottom-group split rather than an exact order. The ~25 percent forecast-scale offset documented for Figures 7 and 8 barely matters here — the ranking is fully scale-robust, and the R^2 VI magnitudes land on the paper's scale (the leader within about 10 percent of the paper's value).

Generated by `src/figure11.py` (doit task `figure11`) from `variable_importance_T12`; the plotted VI table is also written to `_output/figure11_data.parquet`.
