## Description

Replication of Figure 7 of Kelly, Malamud and Zhou (2024), the out-of-sample market timing performance at training window T = 12. Four panels against model complexity c = P/T, one line per ridge shrinkage level log10(z) in {-3, ..., 3} in the paper's own legend colors: Panel A the out-of-sample R^2, Panel B the mean L2 norm of the fitted coefficients, Panel C the timing-strategy expected return, Panel D its volatility (monthly units). The x-axis is broken exactly like the paper's: a linear segment over c in [0, 50] emphasizing the interpolation boundary (dashed line at c = 1), then a break, then a sliver at c in [990, 1000] for the extreme-complexity end; the grid points at c = 64 to 512 fall inside the break (as in the paper) but remain in the exported panel data. Panels A, B, and D pin the paper's y-limits, so the boundary spikes clip exactly as they do in the paper.

The take-away is the paper's namesake pattern: at low shrinkage the coefficient norm and strategy volatility explode at c = 1 and then FALL as complexity grows past the boundary, while expected return rises — so trading performance improves with complexity even though the OOS R^2 stays negative through most of the grid (turning slightly positive only at extreme complexity, as the paper reports). The negative, boundary-collapsing R^2 in Panel A is the point of the paper, not an estimation problem: R^2 is dominated by forecast scale while trading profits load on forecast direction.

Level note: the y-levels of Panels B, C, and D depend on the forecast scale, which under this pipeline's centered RFF standardization sits about 25 percent below the paper's; every scale-free statistic (the Figure 8 Sharpe ratio, information ratio, and alpha t-statistic) matches the paper's anchors within about 1 percent, so the offset is cosmetic, not informational.

Generated by `src/figure7.py` (doit task `figure7`) from `oos_grid_T12`; the plotted panel data is also written to `_output/figure7_data.parquet`.
