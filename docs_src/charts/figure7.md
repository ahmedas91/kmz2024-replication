## Description

Replication of Figure 7 of Kelly, Malamud and Zhou (2024), the out-of-sample market timing performance at training window T = 12. Four panels against model complexity c = P/T on a log axis, one line per ridge shrinkage level log10(z) in {-3, ..., 3} (dark to light in increasing z): Panel A the out-of-sample R^2, Panel B the mean L2 norm of the fitted coefficients, Panel C the timing-strategy expected return, Panel D its volatility (monthly units). The dashed line marks the interpolation boundary c = 1; Panels A, B, and D pin the paper's y-limits, so the boundary spikes clip exactly as they do in the paper.

The take-away is the paper's namesake pattern: at low shrinkage the coefficient norm and strategy volatility explode at c = 1 and then FALL as complexity grows past the boundary, while expected return rises — so trading performance improves with complexity even though the OOS R^2 stays negative. The negative, boundary-collapsing R^2 in Panel A is the point of the paper, not an estimation problem: R^2 is dominated by forecast scale while trading profits load on forecast direction.
