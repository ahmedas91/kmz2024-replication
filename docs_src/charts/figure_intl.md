## Description

The external-validity extension of the replication (issue #16): the full virtue-of-complexity experiment pointed at the developed-ex-US market excess return (Ken French `Developed_ex_US_3_Factors`, Mkt-RF), forecast with the SAME US information set as every other study — the 14 standardized Goyal-Welch predictors plus the target's own lag — through the same engine, grids (T = 12, P up to 12,000, log10(z) in {-3..3} plus ridgeless), and conventions. Sample: 1991-07 to 2020-12 (354 months; the French series starts 1990-07 and the target's 12-month volatility burn-in consumes the first year), 200 seeds.

**Take-away: the shape generalizes, the level does not.** Qualitatively the panels reproduce the VoC signature — the OOS R^2 is negative with the interpolation-boundary collapse, and the Sharpe ratio still rises essentially monotonically with complexity for every shrinkage level. But the levels are a fraction of the US results: at c = 1000 the ridgeless Sharpe is 0.11 and the best heavily-shrunk model (z = 10^3) reaches only 0.15, versus 0.43-0.49 for the US market and corporate bonds on the paper period. With only 341 out-of-sample months, the annualized Sharpe ratio carries a standard error of roughly 0.19, so the international performance is statistically indistinguishable from zero.

Two design caveats, stated up front because they ARE the likely explanation rather than a nuisance: (1) the information set is US predictors (valuation ratios, rates, and spreads of the US market) forecasting non-US returns, so the model can only exploit whatever of that information spills over; (2) the sample is about a third of the market study's 1,092 months, so uncertainty bands are wide and a true modest Sharpe could not be distinguished from zero either. The honest conclusion is that complexity itself transfers (more complexity never hurt), but this information set on this sample produces no measurable international timing skill.

Generated by `src/figure_intl.py` (doit task `figure_intl`) from `oos_grid_intl_T12`, through the shared data-driven renderer (`figure_voc_study.py`) also used by the bonds figure; the plotted rows are written to `_output/figure_intl_data.parquet`. Like every estimation artifact the study is keyed by the sample period; the updated twin (`SAMPLE_END=2024-12`) makes the conclusion starker still — through 2024-12 the ridgeless c = 1000 Sharpe falls to 0.02 (0.05 at z = 10^3), so the 2021-2024 extension erases even the modest paper-period level while the rising-with-complexity shape persists.
