## Description

The visual centerpiece of the Nagel (2025) critique test (issue #17): the estimated lag weights of the replicated VoC forecast (ridgeless, c = 1000, across-seed mean forecast) regressed on the trailing 12 volatility-standardized market returns, drawn against the linearly declining weights of Nagel's recency-weighted momentum benchmark. If the complex model were "just momentum in disguise", the two weight profiles would coincide and the regression would fit tightly.

What the numbers say (paper period): the anatomy regression explains only R^2 = 0.16 of the VoC forecast's variation, so the forecast is NOT simply a recency-weighted average of recent returns - but the critique still half-lands, because the momentum benchmark achieves comparable performance on identical metrics (Sharpe 0.41 vs the VoC row's 0.44, with a larger alpha t-statistic), and the spanning regression of the VoC strategy on the benchmark plus the static market leaves an insignificant alpha (t = 1.8). Honest reading, stated in the report subsection: the VoC strategy's performance is largely spanned by a far simpler strategy, even though its forecast contains more than momentum.

Convention note (checkup finding, deliberate): the VoC metrics row scores the ACROSS-SEED MEAN forecast path, which denoises the forecast and lifts its Sharpe slightly above the average-the-statistics number the Figure 8 cache reports (0.44 vs 0.43). The comparison is apples-to-apples within the table (one realized path, one forecast per strategy), but the row is not directly comparable to the Figure 8 anchor value.

Generated by `src/table_nagel.py` (doit task `nagel`, fed by `doit export_forecasts`) from `nagel_anatomy`; the companion comparison table is `_output/nagel_comparison_table.tex`. Artifacts are keyed by the sample period like every estimation output.
